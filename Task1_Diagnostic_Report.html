1Executive Summary

Purpose

This report documents the diagnostic review for ticket #2437, a delayed rotation response in the RBA-2201 surgical robotic arm reported by Mercy General Hospital. It records measured response times for the three core commands, the suspected cause of the delay, the optimization tested, and the work still needed before the ticket can be closed.

Key findings

  • rotate_joint is the only command over specification. It responds in 0.15 s in simulation against a 0.10 s target, which is 50% over. The hospital reported a delay of 0.18 s in clinical use.
  • move_arm meets its 0.10 s target with no margin. adjust_grip responds in 0.05 s, well inside its 0.09 s target.
  • The tested optimization reduced execution time by 20% across all commands. rotate_joint improved to 0.12 s, but remains 0.02 s over target. Meeting the 0.10 s target requires a reduction of at least 33%.
  • The delay is consistent. Across 30 trials per command, response times varied by less than 1 ms. This points to a fixed processing cost in the command's code path rather than intermittent load or communication problems.

2Issue Description

Problem statement

The ticket reports a delayed response in the surgical robotic arm's rotation function, impacting real-time adjustments. The rotate_joint command is experiencing slower-than-expected response times, with a delay of 0.18 s reported. No troubleshooting had been done before this review. The ticket calls for a diagnostic review of the control code to locate inefficiencies, followed by implementing and testing a solution.

Symptoms and impact

The surgical team observed a noticeable delay when rotating the arm to adjust positioning during procedures. The ticket rates severity as high because delays in response could impact surgical accuracy, particularly in high-stakes scenarios.

  • Lag between surgeon input and joint movement. The instrument keeps moving after the surgeon expects it to stop, or starts after they expect it to.
  • Reduced precision. A delayed rotation makes fine orientation of instruments harder and increases the risk of overshoot near delicate tissue.
  • Surgeon compensation and fatigue. Operators slow down or over-correct to work around the lag, which lengthens procedures and adds cognitive load.
  • Patient safety risk. In time-critical moments, such as reacting to bleeding, a delayed response limits how quickly the surgeon can reposition.

3Diagnostic Process

Initial observations

Before optimization, the commands responded in 0.10 s (move_arm), 0.15 s (rotate_joint) and 0.05 s (adjust_grip). rotate_joint was the slowest command: 1.5 times slower than move_arm and 3 times slower than adjust_grip.

Commands tested

move_arm

Moves the arm to a designated position. Used for larger positioning movements over the surgical site. Target: 0.10 s.

rotate_joint

Rotates the joint to adjust positioning. Used for real-time adjustments during the procedure, so delays here are felt most directly by the surgeon. Target: 0.10 s. Flagged in ticket #2437 with a reported delay of 0.18 s.

adjust_grip

Adjusts the grip for handling precision when grasping or releasing tissue and instruments. Target: 0.09 s.

Hypothesis

The rotate_joint command performs redundant calculations on each call, for example recomputing joint angle transforms or kinematics that could be calculated once or cached. The delay's consistency across trials supports a fixed processing cost in the code path over causes that would vary, such as system load or network latency.

Tools and techniques used

  • Python 3, using the time module to measure response times and NumPy for numerical work.
  • Control System Diagnostic Notebook, run in Jupyter inside VS Code, using the check_response_time and optimized_command functions.
  • Repeated trials: 30 runs per command to confirm the timings were stable and not the result of a single noisy run.
  • Iterative testing: measuring a baseline, applying an optimization, and re-measuring to compare.

Analysis of findings

  • rotate_joint is the bottleneck. It is the only command over target, by 0.05 s (50%), and the slowest in absolute terms.
  • The optimization helped but did not resolve the issue. A uniform 20% reduction gave rotate_joint the largest absolute gain (0.03 s), but it still misses the target by 0.02 s. Reaching 0.10 s from 0.15 s requires a reduction of at least 33%.
  • move_arm had no margin before optimization. At exactly 0.10 s, any added load would push it over target. After optimization it has 0.02 s of headroom.
  • adjust_grip is low priority. It was already 0.04 s inside its target before optimization.
  • Timings are stable. The spread across 30 trials was under 1 ms for every command, so the delay is reproducible and should be straightforward to verify once fixed.

Reported versus simulated delay: the hospital reported a rotate_joint delay of 0.18 s, while the simulation measured 0.15 s. The simulation covers only the control code, so the remaining 0.03 s likely comes from parts of the system it does not model, such as motor response, sensors or communication. Fixing the control code addresses the share of the delay that is in software. Hardware-in-the-loop testing is needed to confirm the full delay on the RBA-2201 falls within target. Note that the diagnostic notebook lists 0.18 s in its "Expected" column; this report treats 0.18 s as the reported delay and 0.10 s as the target.

5Optimizations and Solutions

Code modifications

  • Optimization tested. The notebook's optimized_command() function applies an improvement factor of 0.2 to each command's execution time. This models the expected effect of removing redundant calculations from the command path. The results above are a simulated estimate of that change, not a measured speedup of rewritten control code.
  • Proposed change to rotate_joint. Calculate values that do not change between calls once and cache them, remove repeated calculations within a single call, and move work that is not time-critical, such as logging and validation, out of the command path.
  • Notebook repair. Three documentation cells (Steps 3, 5 and 6) were stored as code cells, which caused a SyntaxError when running the full notebook. They were converted to markdown cells so the notebook runs end to end.
# Simulated optimization used in the diagnostic notebook
def optimized_command(command, improvement_factor=0.2):
    optimized_response_time = check_response_time(command) * (1 - improvement_factor)
    return optimized_response_time

Impact of optimizations and recommended tests

The tested optimization reduced response times by 20%: move_arm from 0.10 s to 0.08 s, rotate_joint from 0.15 s to 0.12 s, and adjust_grip from 0.05 s to 0.04 s. Before the issue can be closed, the following tests are recommended:

  • Profile the real rotate_joint code (for example with cProfile) to confirm where the time is spent, and target a reduction of at least 33%.
  • Re-measure after each code change, using repeated trials and reporting the mean and worst case for each command.
  • Stress test with commands issued in quick succession and in combination, to check that response times hold under realistic surgical workloads.
  • Hardware-in-the-loop testing on a physical RBA-2201, since simulated timings exclude motor, sensor and communication latency. This would also explain the gap between the 0.18 s reported and the 0.15 s simulated.
  • Automated regression tests that fail if any command exceeds its target, so future changes cannot reintroduce the delay.

6Conclusion

Summary of findings

Diagnostics confirmed that rotate_joint is the source of the delay reported in ticket #2437, responding in 0.15 s in simulation (0.18 s reported in clinical use) against a 0.10 s target. The other two commands meet their targets. The delay is consistent across trials, which suggests redundant processing in the command's code path. A 20% optimization reduced rotate_joint to 0.12 s, a meaningful improvement that still falls short of the target.

Overall impact

The tested optimization cuts rotate_joint latency by 0.03 s and gives move_arm headroom it did not have before. For surgeons, this means joint rotations track their input more closely, with less overshoot and less need to compensate. Bringing rotate_joint fully within target would remove the reported lag and improve the arm's reliability for precise, time-critical movements.

Next steps

  • Profile and rewrite the rotate_joint code path to achieve a reduction of at least 33%.
  • Validate the fix through stress testing and hardware-in-the-loop testing on an RBA-2201, and measure how much of the 0.18 s reported delay comes from hardware.
  • Add automated response-time regression tests for all commands.
  • Deploy the fix and follow up with Dr. Emily Chen at Mercy General Hospital to confirm the delay is resolved in clinical use before closing ticket #2437.
